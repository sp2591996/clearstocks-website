How It Works

A quantitative screener for the Nifty 200

This site ranks all 200 stocks in the Nifty 200 index using a machine-learning model trained on historical fundamentals, and separately checks whether each stock looks cheap or expensive versus its own sector right now. Here's exactly how that works, step by step.

STEP 1

Find

Every stock in the Nifty 200 is scored — you can search by name, browse the full list, or jump straight to the screener with filters.

STEP 2

Explore

Each stock has its own page with price, financial ratios, valuation, and the model's rank — so you can see exactly why it landed where it did.

STEP 3

Filter

The screener lets you narrow by sector, valuation, ML score, price movement, and more — or start from a "Growth", "Value" or "Momentum" preset.

STEP 4

Understand

Nothing here is a buy/sell tip. Every score is a data point to research further, not a conclusion — see our disclaimer for the full picture.

Where does the data come from?

Company fundamentals (revenue, profit, debt, capital expenditure, etc.) are pulled from public financial data sources for all Nifty 200 companies. Prices are pulled from market data feeds.

How often is this updated?

The ML rankings and fundamentals are refreshed manually, roughly whenever new quarterly results come out. Prices update automatically through the day (delayed by about 15-20 minutes) during NSE market hours — they are not real-time.

Is this real-time data?

No. Prices are delayed by roughly 15-20 minutes, and the underlying model scores are only as fresh as the last manual refresh. This is a research tool, not a live trading terminal.

Does this site recommend which stocks to buy?

No. The ML score and valuation verdict are analytical outputs of a specific model with specific assumptions and known limitations. They are not investment advice or a recommendation to buy, hold, or sell anything. See the full disclaimer.

What does the ML Score actually measure?

It's a model trained on historical company fundamentals to estimate how a stock's future 3-month return might rank relative to its peers, based on patterns found in past data. Past patterns are not a guarantee of future results.
